Two hundred and fifty-three days. That's how long Cristopher Sánchez waited to chase down a ghost in a Phillies uniform, and last night in San Diego, he caught him. Forty-four and two-thirds scoreless innings, the longest streak in the history of the Philadelphia Phillies, a record that had been etched in stone since 1911 by a man named Grover Cleveland Alexander. Sánchez carved through the Padres lineup for seven more shutout innings, struck out nine, walked nobody, and when he stepped off the mound he pumped his fists like a man who knew exactly what he'd just done. The Phillies won three to nothing. They swept San Diego. And the longest scoreless streak this franchise has ever seen now belongs to a tall lefty who was once an afterthought in a trade with Tampa Bay.

Let's stay in San Diego.

Sánchez's seven brilliant innings extended his scoreless streak to 44 and two-thirds innings, passing Hall of Famer Grover Cleveland Alexander for the Philadelphia Phillies franchise record, in a three-to-nothing win Wednesday against the Padres for a three-game sweep.

Think about that for a second. Alexander set that mark in 1911. The franchise has had Steve Carlton, Cliff Lee, Roy Halladay, Cole Hamels, Curt Schilling, prime Zack Wheeler. Not one of them got there. Sánchez did.

He reached the milestone by getting through the four full innings he needed to pass Alexander, who had a forty-one inning scoreless streak in 1911. He kept going through three more scoreless innings before leaving after throwing one hundred pitches. He allowed six hits, struck out nine and walked none.

The drama wasn't without a few heart attacks along the way.

With Sánchez an inning away from the record, there was a heart-stopping moment as Manny Machado lifted a fly ball to left that Edmundo Sosa caught just in front of the wall leading off the fourth. Sánchez struck out Xander Bogaerts, Ramon Laureano doubled to left, and then the lefty got Jackson Merrill to ground out to second base to set the record.

If you've followed Sánchez over the last calendar year, you understand what this means stylistically. He is not an overpowering ace in the modern sense. He's a sinker-changeup pitcher who lives at the bottom of the zone and trusts his defense. To rack up forty-four scoreless innings as that kind of arm, you need command, sequencing, and a little bit of luck on hard contact. He's had all three.

The offense did what it needed to do behind him.

Trea Turner homered leading off the ninth, his seventh, and Kyle Schwarber hit an RBI single during the two-run sixth.

Turner's been quietly excellent this stretch — that's his sixth or seventh home run depending on which gamelog you trust, and he added his eleventh stolen base of the year.

José Alvarado pitched the ninth for his first save

, which is an interesting little wrinkle. Alvarado closing out a regular season game suggests Rob Thomson is being thoughtful about how he distributes leverage innings with a deep, healthy bullpen. The first save of his season in late May is not a stat to worry about — it's a stat that tells you they didn't need him much before this.

Phillies left-hander Zack Wheeler, four-and-zero with a one-point-six-seven ERA, is scheduled to start Friday night at the Los Angeles Dodgers.

One small footnote on Sánchez before we move on. He's twenty-nine years old. He came over from Tampa Bay in a minor deal in 2019. He didn't make his first All-Star team until 2024. The Phillies have gotten more value out of that trade than almost any move Dave Dombrowski has made in his time in Philadelphia, and Sánchez signed a four-year extension worth twenty-two and a half million dollars in early 2024 that has aged like a wine in a temperature-controlled cellar. So let's talk about that trade, because the clock is now five days from Monday.

We're officially one week out from June 1, 2026 — the date on the NFL calendar that serves as a major financial benchmark, and one that will pave the way for what is a seemingly inevitable trade of wide receiver AJ Brown by the Eagles.

Here's why that date matters in plain English.

If the Eagles trade Brown before June 1, they would incur a $43 million dead cap hit, whereas if they trade the receiver after June 1, the dead cap hit would be just $16.4 million.

The difference is about $27 million in usable cap space this year. That's why nothing has happened yet. That's why everyone is waiting.

Where is he going?

The New England Patriots are the team that Brown is expected to be traded to, according to Sports Illustrated's Albert Breer. This is the worst-kept secret in the NFL, as Brown has been linked to New England for months.

The connection is Mike Vrabel, who was Brown's head coach with the Titans from 2019 through 2021. The Patriots have the cap room, they have the need, they have the leverage point of a coach Brown wants to play for. But it's not done yet.

The two sides are where they had been, which is the Eagles wanting a first-round pick in 2027. The Patriots, the most likely destination, we'll see if someone else surprises, but the Patriots are the most likely destination, not willing to give up a first-round pick as of right now. That means they're not particularly close and that means there's a chance this could drag on for the foreseeable future.

That last part is the only piece of friction here. Howie Roseman wants a 2027 first. New England would prefer to package a 2028 first or a high 2027 pick that isn't a one. From the Eagles' side, getting a 2027 first is the holy grail because that draft is loaded and because Roseman has shown over and over that he values picks in good drafts. If New England holds firm at a 2028 first, that's still a major return — but it's a worse return for the Eagles' planning horizon. So expect this thing to go right down to the wire on Monday or Tuesday.

In the meantime, the Eagles are reshaping the offense around the idea that Brown is not coming back.

They traded up three spots in the first round, with the Cowboys no less, to make sure they jumped ahead of the Steelers and drafted USC receiver Makai Lemon. One of the best receivers in the class, Lemon is a dynamic presence who can handle a lot of responsibility in the offense. Philadelphia also drafted Vanderbilt tight end Eli Stowers in the second round. Ostensibly a tight end, Stowers mostly plays in the slot, and he's a freakish athlete who's a complete mismatch for linebackers and safeties. The Eagles are ready for a post-Brown offense.

And on the field this week, we're already seeing what that looks like.

Rookie first-round pick Makai Lemon got plenty of work with the first-team offense. Lemon likely slots in as the number two receiver behind DeVonta Smith this season, so the Eagles aren't wasting any time getting him reps with Jalen Hurts. Without Dontayvion Wicks on Wednesday, DeVonta Smith, Lemon and Johnny Wilson got the early first-team reps.

Lemon, wearing gold cleats, saw three targets in a four-play span during seven-on-sevens, including a nice catch in traffic in the middle of the field. He also had a focus drop on a slant. He's a rookie. That's what May is for.

The new offensive coordinator is Sean Mannion, and the early reporting from the practice field is that the offense looks meaningfully different.

There were some notable differences. At the top of the list is the under-center usage, which is expected to be higher this season. We already saw that on Wednesday, along with some play action off of it. There was also plenty of motion in team drills too.

That is a substantive shift from what Kellen Moore and Kevin Patullo ran. Under-center, play-action, motion-heavy. That's the kind of design that protects Hurts in the pocket, generates explosive shots downfield, and gives Saquon Barkley clearer downhill lanes. And on cue —

in the first full-team portion of practice, the offensive line opened a big hole for Saquon Barkley and he exploded through it. It's just a non-padded practice in May but Barkley showed he still has plenty of burst.

Let's go to the Sixers, who are not playing basketball, but whose offseason is arguably the most consequential of the Joel Embiid era. As a quick refresher: they finished last regular season as a seven seed, beat Boston in seven games in the first round in one of the great Philadelphia upsets of the decade, then got swept by the Knicks in the second round. Daryl Morey is out as president of basketball operations. Bob Myers, the former Warriors GM, is leading the search for his replacement.

The latest reporting from Marc Stein and Tony Jones at The Athletic centers on Jameer Nelson, who's been on the Sixers' front office for years and is now considered a real candidate for the top job.

Nelson is a legitimate candidate to become the 76ers' next president of basketball operations. Stein reiterates in his latest story that Nelson has been "all but assured of a significant role going forward" regardless of whether or not he lands the top job.

The other names in the mix, per Tony Jones, include Mike Gansey from Cleveland, Nick U'Ren from Golden State, Trent Redden from the Clippers, and Matt Lloyd.

Atlanta denied Philadelphia's request to speak to Hawks GM Onsi Saleh last week.

The job is more attractive than it was a month ago, partly because Tyrese Maxey just made All-NBA third team and VJ Edgecombe just made the All-Rookie first team.

VJ Edgecombe has been named to the 2025-26 NBA All-Rookie First Team after an impressive debut season with the Sixers. Across 75 regular-season games, VJ Edgecombe averaged 16.0 points, 5.6 rebounds, 4.2 assists, 1.4 steals and 0.5 blocks.

That backcourt is now the thing the new president will be expected to build around. The hard part is that Embiid and Paul George have enormous contracts and varying levels of injury risk.

Whoever becomes the Sixers' lead executive has to answer a $299-million question in the total amount of money Philadelphia owes Joel Embiid and Paul George over the next three years.

What does the actual roster need? Frontcourt help, badly. Behind Embiid the depth was thin all year, and when he missed time the team had no answers. The 22nd pick in the upcoming draft could be used on a frontcourt prospect like Henri Veesaar or Chris Cenac Jr., depending on how the board falls. The Sixers' free agents include Kelly Oubre Jr. and Quentin Grimes, both of whom will hit the market.

Grimes's restricted free agency last summer ended with him accepting the Sixers' one-year, $8.7 million qualifying offer. With rookie VJ Edgecombe's emergence as the Sixers' starting shooting guard, Grimes served mainly as the team's sixth man for the 2025-26 campaign. In the regular season, Grimes played 29.4 minutes per game, averaged 13.4 points, 3.6 rebounds and 3.3 assists, and shot 33.4 percent from three-point range.

The financial reality is unforgiving.

Quentin Grimes and Kelly Oubre Jr. will effectively decide which, if any, mid-level exception the Sixers have access to this offseason. If the Sixers cross the first apron, they'll lose access to the $15.0 million non-taxpayer mid-level exception. If they cross the second apron, they won't even have access to the $6.1 million taxpayer MLE. They wouldn't have a mid-level exception at all, so they'd only be able to offer veteran-minimum contracts to external free agents.

So if you bring back Grimes and Oubre at fair-market salaries, you almost certainly lose your tool to add an outside free agent. That's the math problem the new president inherits.

And lurking behind all of it is the question that everyone in Philadelphia is too tired to ask out loud: is this iteration of the Sixers actually championship-viable, or is the right move to make a hard pivot? Trading Embiid or George is the only realistic way to dramatically reshape this roster. The Sixers' front office search isn't just about who runs the team. It's about who decides that question. Bob Myers has said he won't rush the process, but a decision before the June draft would be helpful.

To the Flyers, who finished their season in late May after being swept in the second round by Carolina. That alone is a sentence I didn't think I'd write a year ago.

The Flyers have had three drafts since Danny Brière and Keith Jones took over the front office, all with a slow and steady rebuild in mind, and are headed for their fourth at the end of next month, this time with the 21st overall pick and the status of a breakout playoff team. The offseason questions are clear.

Briere must re-sign restricted free agents Trevor Zegras and Jamie Drysdale. The Hockey News' Sam Carchidi believes the Flyers need a high-end center and a defenseman who can quarterback the power play.

Those two needs — top-six center and power-play quarterback — define the entire summer.

The 2026 NHL Draft is at the end of June. The Flyers pick 21st in the first round, then 53 and 85. The fascinating question is whether Brière packages picks and players to move up.

Heading into the 2026 offseason, the Philadelphia Flyers are equipped with a ton of draft capital and assets, with various paths to explore to make good use of them. And, although they just made the playoffs, it might be wise to continue to invest via the NHL draft. As things currently stand, the Flyers won't be able to make too much happen with the No. 21 overall pick they own, but they could package that to acquire something greater, whether it be a player or another draft pick higher in the order. If the Flyers opt to go for the latter, they won't have to look much further than the Chicago Blackhawks, who are picking fourth overall in the 2026 draft.

That's the spice. If Chicago is genuinely willing to move that fourth-overall pick, the Flyers could get to a top-five selection in a draft headed by Gavin McKenna, with Caleb Malhotra and others right behind. That's a top-six center. That's the missing piece. The cost would be steep — possibly Tyson Foerster or Owen Tippett plus a defenseman like Emil Andrae or Oliver Bonk plus the 21st pick itself. But for a team that just tasted the second round and wants the next step, that's the kind of swing Briere will at least consider.

If they stay at 21, the names floating are Jack Hextall and Casey Mutryn and Ryan Lin, depending on what slides. The Flyers think it's them. We'll see.
