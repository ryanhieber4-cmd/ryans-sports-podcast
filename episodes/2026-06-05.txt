What a difference one starter makes.

Zack Wheeler took a no-hitter into the sixth inning at Citizens Bank Park yesterday afternoon, struck out eight, allowed two hits over seven innings, and the Phillies finished off a sweep of the San Diego Padres with a six-to-four win. They've now beaten San Diego all six times they've played them this season. They've climbed past the Padres in the National League Wild Card standings. And they've gone from ten games under five hundred to a playoff position in thirty-seven days. Buckle up. There is a lot to talk about.

Good morning, Ryan, and welcome to Friday, June fifth. I hope your coffee is hot and your inbox is light, because I'm not sure how you're supposed to spend a workday this morning when your baseball team is suddenly the hottest story in the National League and your basketball team is on the clock for the twenty-second pick in a draft that is now eighteen days away.

Here is what we are working with today. The Phillies, fresh off that sweep of the Padres, are at home tonight to open a three-game set with the Chicago White Sox. Jesús Luzardo gets the ball. The Eagles wrap their voluntary OTA window and head toward mandatory minicamp next week, with the post-A.J.-Brown offense already taking shape on the practice field, and a Brandon Graham wrinkle that won't fully go away. The Sixers do not play, of course, but draft month is officially here, the front office has a new face running it, and Quentin Grimes and Kelly Oubre Junior are sitting on top of an offseason ledger that is going to define the next two years. And the Flyers are deep into a busy offseason of their own, with the Dan Vladar extension reportedly nearly across the finish line and the twenty-first overall pick coming up in the same week as Philadelphia's basketball pick.

Around the leagues, the Knicks lead the NBA Finals one-to-nothing after a Game One comeback in San Antonio, and the Stanley Cup Final, almost unbelievably, is going to leave Raleigh tied at a game apiece after Seth Jarvis ended Game Two in overtime last night.

Let's get to work. Start with the team that's stolen the city.

The Phillies' story right now is a story of two stars at exactly the right time. Wheeler in the rotation, and the bullpen behind him. Yesterday's game was a clean illustration of both.

Wheeler took a no-hitter into the sixth before it was broken up on a bloop single by Bryce Johnson, and finished the day allowing two runs on a Manny Machado homer while striking out eight.

That is, by my count, his fifth consecutive start of at least seven innings and two earned runs or fewer. He is five-and-one. He is pitching like a Cy Young candidate. And the Phillies are now twenty-four-and-ten under interim manager Don Mattingly, who took over a team that, by any reasonable measure, was sinking.

Think about what that means for a second.

They are twenty-four and ten under Mattingly and they've gone from ten games under five hundred to a wild-card position in thirty-seven days.

That is not a hot streak. That is a regime change disguised as a hot streak. The Phillies of late April were a team that couldn't score, couldn't get out of innings, and looked like they had quit on Rob Thomson. The Phillies of early June have a top-of-the-rotation arm pitching like an ace, a bullpen that has a closer ringing bells, and a lineup that just put up six runs against the Padres for, in case you missed the note,

the first time Philadelphia had scored more than four runs since May eighteenth — a span of thirteen games.

That is the part that should still concern you a little. Even in the middle of this run, the offense has been carried by margins and matchups, not by volume.

Bryson Stott, Trea Turner and Alec Bohm had RBI singles yesterday, and the other Philadelphia runs scored on a throwing error by San Diego reliever Yuki Matsui on a pickoff attempt, and a fielder's choice by Brandon Marsh.

That is a six-run game built on singles, mistakes and one big swing. The big swing came from a guy who suddenly looks like the addition the front office hoped he would be.

I'm talking about Adolis García.

García, who signed with Philadelphia in the offseason to provide right-handed power to the lineup, went deep for the first time since May sixth, and his fifth homer of the season came off San Diego starter Lucas Giolito in the fifth inning to give the Phillies a two-to-nothing lead.

Almost a month between home runs is a long time for a guy who was paid to slug. But the Phillies have been patient, García has been working at it, and yesterday it finally arrived in a real spot. If he is even an average power threat the rest of the way, this lineup looks meaningfully different. They no longer have to live on Schwarber solo shots and Turner ground balls finding holes.

There is also a piece of news from a couple of days ago that's worth holding onto. Top Phillies prospect Aidan Miller is going to have a spinal procedure, but, importantly, the reporting out of South Philly suggests he might still play this season. That is a tightrope. Miller is supposed to be the guy waiting in the wings. He is supposed to be the answer at third or short or somewhere in the infield in 2027. So you want him healthy. You want him healthy long-term. But you also do not want this season to be a lost one for his development if he can avoid it. Worth watching.

Tonight, they open the White Sox series.

LHP Jesús Luzardo, who's four-and-four with a four-point-three ERA, starts for the Phillies on Friday against Chicago lefty Anthony Kay, who's five-and-one with a three-seven-seven ERA.

Kay has actually been good. The White Sox have not. This is, on paper, exactly the kind of series the Phillies have to win three of, especially with the Mets cooling off and the Wild Card race compressing every single day.

Luzardo is the pitcher I am most curious about over these next four weeks. When the Phillies traded for him in the offseason, the bet was simple. He'd be a Wheeler-tier arm if he stayed healthy. He has not been that. He has had stretches of brilliance and stretches where every fastball lives middle. Tonight against a team that is, generously, rebuilding, is a chance for him to give the Phillies six innings of clean baseball and let the bullpen handle the rest. And the bullpen, as the Inquirer noted yesterday, has become the heartbeat of this turnaround. The phrase being used around the clubhouse is that when Jhoan Duran comes in, the players literally feel like the game is over. That is exactly what good teams say about a great closer.

One more Phillies note before we move on. Cole Hamels is going to be at Citizens Bank Park as part of a charity "Sluggball" event, taking some swings, doing some media. I know that's not on-field news, but for a fan base that has been through a lot with that core, seeing Hamels back at the park in any capacity, in this kind of atmosphere, with this team playing this kind of baseball, is a nice marker of where things sit. The vibes, as the kids say, are immaculate.

Phillies record this morning: twenty-nine and twenty-seven, second in the NL East, holding the third Wild Card. The version of this team I expected in March is finally on the field. We will see if it can hold.

Let's move to the NovaCare. Or, well, the Jefferson Health Training Complex.

The Eagles' offseason has been defined, more than anything else, by the A.J. Brown trade.

Philadelphia officially sent Brown to the Patriots for a 2028 first-round draft pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick

, ending a saga that had hovered over the entire spring like weather you couldn't dodge. Howie Roseman has spoken about why he ultimately accepted New England's offer, the message from the building has been about moving forward, and the new wide receiver room — Makai Lemon, Hollywood Brown, Elijah Moore, Dontayvion Wicks, plus, of course, DeVonta Smith — is now front and center.

Here is what I find most interesting, beyond the trade itself. The Eagles' first open practice since the deal showed an offense that is, by design, very different from the one we have watched for the last five years.

The Eagles' offense is expected to look very different under new offensive coordinator Sean Mannion, and that was definitely the case in the one practice reporters got to see — Mannion used the word "blend" plenty of times in his introductory press conference, but the Eagles didn't hire him to run the same stale offense we saw in 2025, and you should expect to see plenty of the staples from a Shanahan scheme in 2026: more under-center usage, more motion and a zone-blocking run scheme.

Read that twice if you have to. The Eagles are essentially admitting, with the hire of Mannion and with what they have already installed, that the late-season offense in 2025 was broken and that the fix is a full philosophical overhaul. More under center. More motion. A zone-blocking run game. That is not a tweak. That is a different play sheet.

The interesting part is whether Jalen Hurts and Saquon Barkley fit. Hurts has thrived under center in stretches before, but the Eagles in 2024 and 2025 leaned heavily on shotgun, RPO, designed quarterback runs. Barkley, meanwhile, is the best running back in football right now, and the small concern with a strict zone-blocking scheme is whether Barkley's freelance, vision-based style gets a little squeezed. The PhillyVoice piece I read yesterday made the point pretty bluntly. Tank Bigsby's one-cut style is actually a better fit for what Mannion wants to run than Barkley's freelance method. Of course Barkley is still the bell cow. Nobody is taking touches away from him. But it is a real coaching question — how do you build a zone-blocking run scheme that doesn't force Saquon to color inside lines he has never colored inside?

The on-field practice notes are pretty rich, too. The first OTA session reporters watched had

the Eagles wasting no time getting Makai Lemon with the first team — the rookie, who was wearing gold cleats, saw plenty of targets during 7-on-7s before the team drills.

That's exactly what I'd want to see. Lemon was their first-round pick. He needs every rep he can get this summer because, whether the Eagles want to admit it or not, he is going to be expected to give them real snaps as the second or third receiver in Week One.

A couple of other notes from those practices. With Lane Johnson sitting out — veteran rest, not an injury concern — rookie third-rounder Markel Bell got first-team reps at right tackle. Bell played left tackle at Miami, and the Eagles are starting him on the right side, which is the side Johnson has owned for a decade. That depth chart, especially for the long term, matters more than people realize. Lane is not going to play forever. Whoever wins the swing tackle reps this summer is the first guy off the bench if anything happens up front.

On the defensive side, two things worth tracking. One — Jihaad Campbell is rehabbing a shoulder, his second shoulder procedure in as many seasons. That is the kind of soft red flag you note in the file. Two — Cooper DeJean is moonlighting at safety in base packages, sliding back to nickel in sub. The fact that DeJean is even being asked to do that tells you the Eagles trust him as much as any defensive back on the roster. He is going to be a star.

And then the Brandon Graham wrinkle. The Eagles released Graham on June 2 as a post-June 1 cut, a procedural move based on how his contract was structured when he came out of retirement last year. The reporting around it is that this isn't necessarily the end of the Brandon Graham era. The ball is in Graham's court. He can re-sign. He can walk away. He can do what feels right. But the longest-tenured Eagle in franchise history, the man who recovered from a re-torn triceps to play in Super Bowl LIX, is now technically not on the roster as of yesterday. That is a hell of a sentence to read on a Friday morning.

Around the periphery: Josh Sweat trade rumors got cold water poured on them earlier this week. The Eagles called the Panthers about possibly trading up to the nineteenth pick before the draft, which is a tell about how Roseman valued certain players in the back end of the first round. And Andy Dalton, somewhat quietly, has been working as Hurts's primary backup over Tanner McKee through OTAs. McKee is in his fourth year. He has been the post-draft darling of every Eagles film analyst on Twitter. And the team has, for now, slotted him third. That decision is not made yet. But it is interesting.

Mandatory minicamp is Tuesday and Wednesday of next week, June ninth and tenth. Then the team breaks until training camp in late July. We will have a real read on this offense by the back end of next week. Until then, we are reading tea leaves.

Let's go to the Sixers, where the news is all about what's coming and what's being decided behind closed doors.

There were no games last night, obviously — they have not played in weeks — but the NBA Finals are happening, and that always sharpens the focus on what the Sixers have to do to get there.

NBA draft month is here, and the Sixers now have a president of basketball operations in Mike Gansey.

Gansey is the new voice in the room replacing Daryl Morey, and his first draft is in eighteen days, on Monday, June twenty-third.

The Sixers own the twenty-second overall pick. They do not own anything in the lottery, which is one of the very real costs of the Jared McCain trade in February. Now, in fairness to the front office, they did get a haul — three second-round picks plus the Rockets' first-rounder, which landed at twenty-two — and they also acquired Houston's pick by routing it through Oklahoma City. But it does mean their best shot at adding a meaningful long-term piece to this Maxey-Edgecombe backcourt before twenty-twenty-eight is either this pick at twenty-two, or trading up.

There is a very interesting idea floating around about trading up. The Thunder, fresh off losing the conference finals in seven games to the Spurs, own the Clippers' pick at number twelve as well as the Sixers' own pick at number seventeen. Oklahoma City is the only team in the league with both the picks and the roster crunch to back out of this draft if they don't love a guy at twelve. The Sixers, in theory, could package the Clippers' twenty-twenty-eight unprotected first they got in the James Harden deal and try to climb. That is not a small ask. The Clippers' twenty-eight first is one of the most valuable trade chips in the league. But if Gansey believes there is a player at twelve worth jumping for, that's the asset that moves the needle.

The other big Sixers story is free agency. Quentin Grimes and Kelly Oubre Junior are both unrestricted. The Sixers have, frankly, two ways to keep both: pay a lot of luxury tax, or convince at least one to take a discount. Neither is likely. League insiders, according to PhillyVoice reporting, recognize the Sixers are in a real pickle here. If they let one walk, they get to use the taxpayer mid-level exception. If both walk, they could open up the full non-taxpayer mid-level — bigger money, longer term — but at the cost of a hard cap at the first apron. None of those scenarios feel like an upgrade. All of them feel like a sideways step on a team that needs a forward step.

The good news, if you want it, is that the building blocks are there. Maxey is locked in. VJ Edgecombe coming off a rookie year that, by all accounts, was exactly what they hoped for. Embiid is still Embiid, when he plays. Paul George is still on the books. The path to a deep playoff run is narrow. But it exists, and the Gansey hire suggests ownership wants to keep walking down it.

One last note for Sixers fans, in the gallows-humor file. The 2025-26 season ended with what the Sixers themselves described as a second-round sweep by the Knicks. The same Knicks who, last night, took Game One of the Finals against the Spurs. So if anyone tries to tell you Philadelphia had a chance against this version of New York — yeah, no. The Knicks are real. The Knicks were real then. That's not your front office's fault. That's just a hard playoff bracket.

To the Flyers. Where, frankly, this is one of the most interesting Philadelphia offseasons in a decade.

The Flyers got back into the playoffs for the first time since 2020 this past year. They beat the Penguins in six in the first round. They lost to the Hurricanes in four in the second round. And what's been most striking about this offseason, more than any single move, is the level of optimism around the team for the first time in years. The Hockey Writers framed it well — there is genuine momentum, perhaps at its highest level since the early 2010s. That is a real statement.

The biggest piece of news from this week is the Dan Vladar contract. According to the reporting, a Vladar extension is, quote, "pretty much done." The numbers being floated are a five-year extension worth somewhere between five and a half and six million annually. That feels right. Vladar was excellent in Philadelphia's playoff run, and the goaltending tandem of Vladar and Sam Ersson — assuming Ersson is healthy — gives the Flyers more stability between the pipes than they've had since Carter Hart was the guy. Speaking of Hart, by the way, he is in this Stanley Cup Final as Vegas's goaltender. Hockey is a small world.

The other key piece is the draft. The Flyers own the twenty-first pick on the night of June twenty-sixth. The Hockey Writers had a piece this week looking at five prospects they could take there. There's also a separate piece — and this is one of those names you will hear a lot over the next three weeks — about a six-foot-four forward with "goal-scoring hands" being a possible Flyer at twenty-one. Danny Brière and the front office have been pretty open about the fact that they want to use this draft window, while they still have two firsts in one of the next two summers thanks to the Scott Laughton trade, to take some swings on upside.

Beyond Vladar and the draft, the Flyers have business to do.

The Flyers have thirteen restricted free agents this summer, a much higher number than most fans would think.

Most of them are depth players. Two of them, Trevor Zegras and Jamie Drysdale, are not. The reporting from The Athletic's Kevin Kurz is that there is nothing imminent on the Zegras extension despite some chatter, and that it'll eventually get done but it isn't done yet. Drysdale is the same kind of patient negotiation. Both players were drafted by Anaheim, where, league agents have famously said, contracts are not easy. That history seems to be carrying over to Philadelphia, at least in terms of how long these things take.

The Flyers also signed Jacob Gaucher to a one-year, eight-fifty deal, which is housekeeping but worth noting. They added forward Ilya Pautov from their 2024 draft class on a three-year entry-level. Dev camp starts on June twenty-ninth. Training camp opens September seventeenth.

The Flyers are going to be a more talked-about team next season. Bank on it.

Let's go around the leagues.

The NBA Finals. The Knicks took Game One in San Antonio, one-oh-five to ninety-five, and the way it happened tells you everything you need to know about this New York team.

New York overcame early offensive struggles and a fourteen-point third-quarter deficit Wednesday night to secure a gritty 105-95 Game One victory over the San Antonio Spurs in the NBA Finals, and with the win in San Antonio, the Knicks steal home-court advantage in the series.

Jalen Brunson dropped thirty, including thirteen in the fourth quarter, despite shooting twelve-for-thirty-one. Karl-Anthony Towns added eighteen and twelve. And the Knicks held Victor Wembanyama to six-of-twenty-one shooting, even though he still had twenty-six and twelve. That is the recipe. That is what a championship team looks like — winning a game when your superstar shoots thirty-nine percent and the other team's superstar puts up a near-triple-double. Game Two is tonight on ABC, eight-thirty Eastern.

Over to the NHL. The Stanley Cup Final is now tied at one game apiece after the Hurricanes pulled off a miracle in Game Two last night.

Carolina was facing a 2-0 deficit on home ice midway through the third period when they stormed back to take a 3-2 lead, and after a late goal from Golden Knights captain Mark Stone tied the score, Seth Jarvis notched the overtime winner with a power-play tally.

Carolina now has a split before the series shifts to Las Vegas for Game Three on Saturday. The other story from Game One, by the way, was Mitch Marner's game-saving shot block in the dying seconds. Marner has been a different player in this Vegas uniform — seven goals, fifteen assists in the playoffs — and he is putting on a postseason that, if Vegas wins, is going to put him squarely in Conn Smythe conversation. We're going to keep an eye on this one. A best-of-five from here.

In Major League Baseball, the other big result yesterday was Arizona walking off the Dodgers three-to-two.

The Diamondbacks defeated the Dodgers 3-2 on June 4, and Arizona was down 2-0 heading into the bottom of the eighth — but in the eighth, a Corbin Carroll home run and a Geraldo Perdomo RBI single evened things up, and in the ninth, Ketel Marte's eleventh home run finished out the victory.

The Dodgers had six hits. Ohtani did not play. The NL West, which everyone had pegged as a Dodger runaway in March, is suddenly a four-team race. The Cubs also came back from a five-run deficit to beat the Athletics seven-to-six. The Yankees, Orioles and Blue Jays all won. Just nine games on the schedule yesterday — a light midweek slate before everybody gets back to a full board tonight.

NFL news this morning. The Eagles trade we covered already. The bigger national story is that the Browns and Rams made a Myles Garrett trade — yes, the Myles Garrett trade — that involves the Rams sending edge rusher Jared Verse the other way. That deal has been the talk of the league for the last twenty-four hours, with Bill Barnwell and others on the national side trying to figure out who actually won it. As of this morning, ESPN was still publishing analysis. The early conclusion seems to be that the Browns at least came out with their dignity and a star player back, but the Rams now get Garrett, and Garrett, even at thirty, is one of the best defensive players in football. The NFC West is going to be a problem.

A.J. Brown spoke after the trade, saying he feels close to "heaven" joining the Patriots and reuniting with head coach Mike Vrabel. Make of that what you will. Pittsburgh's Alex Highsmith — sorry, Nick Herbig — just got a four-year, one-hundred-million-dollar deal from the Steelers, which is the kind of number that will affect Eagles edge negotiations in 2027 when Nolan Smith's contract comes up. And the Bengals' Ja'Marr Chase said on a podcast yesterday that expectations are raised in Cincinnati because of a better defense. We'll see.

Last MLB note. The Pirates beat the Astros five-to-one yesterday, and the Pittsburgh story remains one of the best in baseball — a young team, a homegrown rotation, suddenly in the Wild Card conversation. The Orioles beat the Red Sox eight-to-two. The Yankees beat the Guardians two-to-one. The Giants and Brewers had a wild one in San Francisco, twelve-to-nine Giants. The Royals beat the Twins. The Blue Jays beat the Braves seven-to-two. That last one matters in the NL East, by the way. Atlanta is no longer running away with anything. The Mets are slumping. The door is wide open for the Phillies to push, and the Brewers — who lost yesterday — are the team to watch in the central.

Let me leave you with this. Thirty-seven days ago, the Phillies were ten games under five hundred. The Eagles were embroiled in an A.J. Brown trade saga that nobody had a good answer for. The Sixers were trying to figure out who would replace Daryl Morey. The Flyers were in the middle of a second-round series they were not expected to win much of anyway. Today, the Phillies are over .500 with the hottest pitcher in the National League. The Eagles have moved on, hired a new coordinator, drafted a wide receiver, and started OTAs. The Sixers have a new president of basketball ops and a draft in eighteen days. And the Flyers are about to extend a goaltender and pick twenty-first overall in a draft class everyone in the city is excited about.

That is what a Philadelphia June looks like when things are going right. Every team is interesting. Every team has a next thing. And tonight at Citizens Bank Park, Jesús Luzardo gets a chance to keep this Phillies story going against a team they should beat.
