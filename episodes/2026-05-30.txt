The problem, as everyone in the front office knows, is that voting Embiid off the island doesn't make him moveable.

Now to the Flyers.

The Flyers are doing something this offseason that they have not done in five years. They are operating like a playoff team.

The Philadelphia Flyers ended their five-year playoff drought by making the playoffs for the first time since 2020.

They beat the Penguins in the first round. They lost to the Hurricanes in four in the second round. They walked out of the season with a top-ten prospect pipeline, real cap space, and a clear set of needs.

Danny Brière's checklist has three things at the top. A top-six center. A top-pair defenseman. And a power play that has somehow been at the bottom of the NHL for five years across three coaching staffs.

Coach Rick Tocchet joked — or maybe it wasn't a joke — that he would spend all summer working on figuring out how to get the power play out of the NHL's basement. That's a tall task considering it's a problem that has persisted for five years and across three coaching staffs. But now it's become critical to fix, because an effective power play could have helped the Flyers win playoff games instead of contributing to losses.

That last sentence is the entire offseason in a clause. The Carolina Hurricanes series was decided in part by special teams. The Flyers have the personnel to be a competent five-on-five team. They cannot be a Cup contender — or even a consistent playoff team — without a functional power play. Trevor Zegras was a bright spot, racking up twenty-three power-play points and landing him in the company of Sidney Crosby and Jack Hughes. But the structure around him needs to be torn down and rebuilt.

The center hunt is going to dominate the early summer. Daily Faceoff's reporting is clear that

the Philadelphia Flyers' most pressing need is a top-six center – and the team knows it too. While GM Danny Briere and president of hockey operations Keith Jones have done an admirable job overseeing the rebuild the last 36 months, bringing in a top pivot has been one area where they have fallen short. As several executives have expressed off the record, the argument could be made that half the league is looking for centers capable of playing higher up the lineup. Simply put: there are very few available pivots on the market.

Mike Sielski of the Inquirer floated Shane Wright of Seattle as a possible target. Daily Faceoff put Matty Beniers and Dylan Cozens on the list. The price for any of these guys, in a market where centers are gold, is going to be steep.

The cap picture is finally working in the Flyers' favor.

The salary cap is rising from $95.5 million to $104 million in 2026-27. Already under the cap this past season, the Flyers will have room to play with come July 1. Even after signing their restricted free agents, they should still have a decent amount of money left over. According to PuckPedia, as of May 20, the Flyers' roster has a projected cap hit of $65,592,917 with projected space of $38,407,083.

Thirty-eight million in projected space. They will not spend all of it. But they have the flexibility to make a major trade and a meaningful free-agent addition for the first time in years.

The draft is coming up too.

Previously, the Flyers were in the lottery, but this year, they're at number 21 overall for their first of five picks through the seven rounds.

The Inquirer's prospect coverage has been excellent — fifteen names ranging from Casey Mutryn to Marek Cali to Maxim Dagenais. Brière likes Canadians, likes centers, and likes developmental projects. Whoever they take at twenty-one will fit that mold.

One last Flyers note. The Stanley Cup Final starts next week. Two of the four conference finalists were eliminated by teams the Flyers had beaten or pushed. Watching the Hurricanes play for the Cup against Vegas, knowing your team gave Carolina a fight in round two, is the kind of thing that builds a fan base's confidence. The Flyers aren't there yet. But they are closer than they have been in a long, long time.
