Let's start in South Philly. The Phillies have flipped a switch, and I am not sure even they fully understand how it happened.

They have won eight of nine and are now sixteen and four under interim manager Don Mattingly, who took over last month after Rob Thomson was fired.

Sixteen and four. Read that number again. That is the pace of a team that wins one hundred and thirty games over a full season. It is not real and it is not sustainable, but it tells you something important, which is that this roster — the same one that limped into May — has been functioning closer to its talent ceiling than at any point in the calendar year.

Last night was a textbook example of how the wins are coming.

Kyle Schwarber, the majors' home run leader, was a noticeable absence. He was under the weather when he woke up, Mattingly said, and so Schwarber was held out of the lineup.

Bryce Harper slid into the designated hitter's role with Alec Bohm playing first base and Edmundo Sosa getting the start at third base against lefty Nick Lodolo.

That is a fundamentally different shape to the lineup. And it still worked.

Andrew Painter started, and he was fine — not crisp, not bad.

Cincinnati got back-to-back singles to begin the top of the second inning against Painter. Tyler Stephenson followed up with a single of his own just down the first base line on a ball off the end of the bat and it trickled into right field for an RBI.

The Phillies clawed back in front in the sixth on a Bohm homer.

Then with Philadelphia going to the bullpen to start the seventh, Sal Stewart welcomed Brad Keller to the game with an opposite field home run to tie things up.

And the eighth got ugly.

Spencer Steer's RBI double with two outs in the eighth off reliever José Alvarado had given the Reds a short-lived four-to-three lead. Orion Kerkering finished the eighth for Alvarado and was credited with the win.

That set up the moment of the night.

On the second pitch to Bryson Stott, Graham Ashcraft hung a slider and Stott crushed it three hundred and sixty feet into the seats in right field for a go-ahead two-run home run.

Philadelphia had scratched MLB home run leader Kyle Schwarber due to illness, and Stott was moved up to the number six spot in a shuffled lineup and hit his fifth homer of the season.

Then Duran shut the door. That is what good teams do. The star is out, the bullpen blows a one-run lead, and somebody else, somewhere else in the order, picks up the slack.

The Schwarber update, Ryan, is the thing to monitor today.

Though Schwarber was probably due for a breather sooner or later after starting in each of the Phillies' forty-seven games of the season, his absence from the lineup wasn't a planned rest day. At the same time, Mattingly didn't indicate that Schwarber's illness is an overly serious concern, so the slugger could be available to pinch hit Monday and potentially return to the lineup Tuesday.

As for Schwarber, he is day-to-day. He has twenty homers in forty-seven games. He had nine in an eight-game span that concluded Friday, when he hit two in Pittsburgh.

The pace he is on is not just a Phillies story, it is a baseball story. He is single-handedly carrying an offense that, on a lot of nights, has not been all that potent. The fact that they won last night without him is a feather in this team's cap.

There was a roster shuffle, too.

The Phillies made a transaction before the game, swapping right-handed hitters Otto Kemp and Felix Reyes. Kemp came back from Triple-A — where he was twenty-two for eighty-four with three homers and twenty-one RBIs in twenty-four games — and started in left field. Reyes was six for thirty-eight with a homer in twelve games with the big club.

"The younger guys, it's hard for them to sit there and not get the at-bats," Mattingly said.

That is also a Mattingly thing — playing the matchups, prioritizing the rhythm of his bench guys. There is an honesty to the way he manages that I think this clubhouse needed.

The other interesting piece of organizational news yesterday was buried in the recap.

Right-handed pitcher Gage Wood, the team's top pick in the 2025 draft, was promoted from low-A Clearwater to Double-A Reading on Monday. The Phillies have been aggressive with young relievers before. In 2023, Orion Kerkering went from low-A to the majors in a single season.

Translation — if this team needs bullpen help by July, do not be shocked if Wood's name shows up faster than you'd expect.

Tonight, it is the showcase matchup of the series.

The Reds will send right-hander Chase Burns, five and one with a one point eight seven ERA, against Phillies left-hander Jesús Luzardo, three and three with a five point oh seven.

Burns is the kind of young arm — high velocity, real swing-and-miss — that this Phillies lineup has had trouble with all year. Luzardo, meanwhile, has had a strange season. The peripherals are better than the ERA. He has had a couple of clunkers stretched out among some genuinely good starts. The thing to watch is whether he can get into the sixth without a meltdown inning. He has been a five-and-fly guy lately, and that puts strain on a bullpen that is being asked to cover a lot of innings.

Big picture on the Phillies — and this is the part I keep coming back to — the run they are on has happened without their best hitter being healthy every day, without their full rotation, and with a manager who has been in the chair for a matter of weeks. Whatever the ceiling of this group turns out to be, the floor just rose. And when you are a team built to win a division and run hot in October, raising the floor in May is more important than just about anything else.

Over to the Eagles, where we are in that strange offseason in-between — the draft is done, the schedule is out, and the first practice is one week away.

Organized Team Activities will begin for the Eagles on May twenty-sixth at the Jefferson Health Training Complex, and the work of an offense that has undergone a significant offseason overhaul will receive the brightest of spotlights.

Here is the thing about this offseason for the Eagles. The defense is the defense — Vic Fangio is back, the front seven added Jonathan Greenard, there is depth at corner.

The good news for the Eagles? Vic Fangio isn't going anywhere. There were rumors that Fangio would retire this offseason, but he's coming back for 2026.

That is settled. The whole season hinges on the offense, and specifically on how Jalen Hurts handles yet another scheme change. New coordinator, new pass-game coordinator, new quarterbacks coach.

Mannion, a thirty-four-year-old first-year coordinator and play-caller, is surrounded by experienced offensive coaches. Besides Sirianni, the Eagles hired Josh Grizzard to be the pass game coordinator and Jerrod Johnson to be a senior offensive analyst. The two of them were candidates for the coordinator job, too. Parks Frazier, who was the pass game coordinator, is now the quarterbacks coach, and has play-calling experience.

That is a lot of voices in Hurts's ear. The Inquirer's beat crew wrote this morning about whether it amounts to too many cooks. Probably not — these guys are all on the same page philosophically, that is why Howie Roseman hired them — but it is something to watch. Hurts has not had continuity. He had Shane Steichen, then Brian Johnson, then Kellen Moore, and now Kevin Patullo's replacement in Mannion. You can be the most talented quarterback in the world and that is still a tax to pay. The thing every Eagles fan should be watching for in OTAs is not who is throwing it well — it is whether Hurts looks comfortable, whether the operation is clean, whether the protections and audibles look like a guy who knows the system cold.

The other big OTA storyline is the A.J. Brown situation, which has been simmering since the playoff loss.

Brown is scheduled to count for $23,393,497 on the Eagles' salary cap this season. If he is traded this offseason before June 1, Brown would instead count for $43,515,106 on the Eagles' cap in dead money. The dead cap charge would still be $43,515,106 if he's traded after June 1, but $27,161,609 of it would count toward the 2027 cap. Though the Eagles have the cap space to trade Brown today if they wanted to, it is more likely that any such trade would happen after June 1.

So this is the next pressure point. They drafted a wide receiver in the first round, twentieth overall, and the language coming out of the Eagles' building since the draft has been pointed.

The Eagles' selection of WR Makai Lemon with the number twenty overall pick in the 2026 NFL Draft provides further indication that the team is preparing to move on from Brown.

Whether Brown reports for OTAs or works out on his own — those are voluntary sessions — will be a tell. Either way, the dollars get cleaner after June first.

A name we are going to mention a lot in the next couple weeks — Cole Payton.

The North Dakota State quarterback has drawn comparisons to Taysom Hill. One longtime quarterback coach even said this of the rookie last month — "Cole Payton is no Taysom Hill, he's better." What does Mannion, an innovative offensive mind, do with a player like that? How does this impact the Tush Push?

That is a fun question. The Tush Push survives — for now — and Hurts is still the offensive engine. But if Payton is genuinely a hybrid weapon, you can see Mannion getting creative with him on the goal line, on third-and-short, in the red zone. The Eagles have hoarded skill-position quirks for years. He fits the pattern.

Defensively, the under-the-radar story to track in OTAs is Jalyx Hunt.

Hunt opened last season as a starter before moving toward a more rotational role. But that's where he can continue to excel. The Eagles brought in Jonathan Greenard, Arnold Ebiketie and Joe Tryon-Shoyinka this offseason. Philadelphia lost Jaelan Phillips to the Carolina Panthers in free agency, so Hunt may have a larger role in 2026.

That edge rotation is one of the deepest groups in the NFL on paper. Fangio's track record with deep front sevens speaks for itself.

And the schedule, finally, is real. The Eagles open at home against Washington on September thirteenth. They go to London in Week five to play the Jaguars. They are in Dallas on Thanksgiving afternoon. They have a Christmas Eve Thursday game against Houston on Amazon. Six primetime games total.

Week 1 vs. Washington Commanders, 4:25 p.m. Sept. 13 on Fox. Week 2 at Tennessee Titans, 1 p.m. Sept. 20 on Fox. Week 3 at Chicago Bears, 8:15 p.m. Sept. 28 on ESPN.

A short turnaround into a Monday night in Chicago in Week three. That whole September stretch is going to tell you a lot about whether the new offense has actually integrated or whether it is going to take until October to get humming.

Last thing on the Eagles — Sirianni made an interesting decision about the workout volume itself.

The Eagles are hosting two mandatory workouts and six voluntary OTAs this offseason, which all start May 26. Last offseason, Philly had three mandatory workouts and six voluntary OTAs.

One fewer mandatory day. Doesn't sound like much, but it is the head coach making a deliberate choice to give players more recovery time before camp. Whether it works will obviously be judged in November, not May.

To the Sixers, where the offseason has already started ugly and is poised to get uglier. The big news of last week — Daryl Morey is out, at least in his prior capacity. Mike Sielski put it cleanly.

The Sixers fired Daryl Morey on Tuesday and have turned over player-personnel power, at least for now and perhaps for longer, to Bob Myers. Nothing will change. The Sixers could have fired Nick Nurse. Nothing would have changed.

That is a brutal indictment from one of the better columnists in this market, and the reason he can write it is the structure of the roster. You cannot meaningfully alter this team without moving Joel Embiid or Paul George, and both contracts are, frankly, among the worst in the league.

Here is the math on why.

The issue is that Philadelphia will have no cap space to work with. The massive money owed to Embiid, fifty-eight point one million, George, fifty-four point one million, and Maxey, forty point eight million, will take up around one hundred and fifty-three million next season. That likely leaves the non-taxpayer mid-level exception or the taxpayer MLE available, depending on who they re-sign and for how much.

That is it. That is the entire toolbox. A mid-level exception and minimum contracts. You cannot meaningfully reshape a roster with that.

Compounding things — they have free agents who actually played meaningful minutes.

Oubre, reserve guard Quentin Grimes, and center Andre Drummond are unrestricted free agents, while Barlow, three point four million, Watford, two point eight million, and Terry, two point six million, have team options for 2026-27.

Oubre started. Grimes was in the rotation. If both walk, the depth gets even thinner.

The Embiid availability question is the one that just keeps coming back.

Since George was signed to a four-year, two-hundred-and-twelve-million max contract two years ago, the same offseason Embiid was given a three-year, one-hundred-and-ninety-three-million extension through 2028-29, Embiid, George and Maxey have hardly been on the court together. George has played seventy-eight regular-season games with the 76ers, including just thirty-seven this year, in part because of a twenty-five-game suspension for violating the league's antidrug policy. Across the past two regular seasons, the star trio has played thirty-six games and seven hundred and forty-two minutes together — just twenty-two percent of Philadelphia's games and less than ten percent of its total minutes.

Twenty-two percent. Ten percent of minutes. For roughly ninety-four percent of the salary. It is an unworkable equation.

And yet, the reporting consensus is that they are stuck with it.

Windhorst made clear that Philadelphia's future remains centered around Embiid and George as the franchise looks toward next season after another early playoff exit.

Multiple years remain on Embiid and George's max contracts, which the Sixers could explore trading but are considered extremely difficult to move, given those players' health struggles and age.

So Bob Myers, in whatever capacity he is operating, walks into a job where the marquee assets are not really assets anymore. The good news, and there is some, is that VJ Edgecombe was a hit as a rookie, Tyrese Maxey is established as a franchise-caliber lead guard, and there is a young core to bend toward. The bad news is that bending toward it requires either a buyer for one of those big contracts — and the Sielski column doesn't see one — or patience until those deals get closer to expiry. Patience and the Sixers historically do not mix.

Schedule-wise for them, there are no games to talk about. The Knicks dispatched them in four. The conference finals on the East side start tonight in New York with Cleveland visiting. That is the team that finished ahead of the Sixers all year — the Cavs — and to be honest, Cleveland looks like the second-best team in the entire NBA right now. The Sixers' offseason is going to play out against that backdrop. It is not fun.

To the Flyers, who I think might be the most interesting Philly team to follow over the next ten weeks. Danny Brière held his end-of-season press conference on Thursday, and the tone was different from past years.

For the first time since he was officially handed the reins of the Flyers three years ago, the general manager used the word rebuild only twice. That's it.

He agreed the team is still growing, but the framing shifted. They made the playoffs. They beat the Penguins in the first round. They pushed Carolina to four games, two of them in overtime, in the second round.

The Flyers earned respect for their resiliency and heart in a ninety-eight-point regular season, followed by a first-round victory over the archrival Pittsburgh Penguins.

The offseason agenda from Brière is pretty clear. Center remains the top priority. Defense is right behind it. The Inquirer's Gustav Elvin laid out the realistic targets.

There are ways Brière could creatively add a top-line pivot this summer. A move for Auston Matthews, if he tells Toronto he wants a trade in the coming weeks, seems unrealistic given Matthews' interests and the fact he can all but pick his next destination. But established top-of-the-lineup centers and former All-Stars Robert Thomas, Nico Hischier, and Matty Beniers all could be available for the right price.

Sielski floated another name.

From Trevor Zegras to David Jiříček, from Owen Tippett-for-Claude Giroux to Jamie Drysdale-for-Cutter Gauthier, Brière has either supported or consummated trades for talented young players who were underperforming with other teams. If Brière sticks to that formula this summer, here's a possible name for him to consider — Seattle Kraken center Shane Wright.

That is the Brière fingerprint. Buy low on a player whose original team has soured on him. Bet on the bounceback. Whether that is Wright or somebody else, you should expect a trade more than a free-agent splash.

The free agent market in 2026 is quite thin, so any impact acquisition will have to come via an RFA signing or a trade.

The Matvei Michkov situation deserves its own segment, frankly, and we will dig deeper as the summer goes. The short version — Michkov had a frustrating end to the season, he was benched at times, and Brière indicated this week that he expects Michkov in Voorhees for at least part of the summer.

Michkov's offseason training plan, and continued immersion in speaking English without needing a translator, will fully be determined in upcoming weeks. Brière said on Thursday that Michkov will at least spend part of his summer in Voorhees.

The Flyers organization continues to publicly express full faith in him. The fanbase, less so. We will see.

Goaltending — Vladar is back next year on the existing deal, and the team has talked about extending him, but the immediate need is a backup capable of starting twenty-five-plus games.

Briere will be looking to add a tandem goaltender partner for Vladar next season. The Flyers exceeded expectations this season getting to the second round of the Stanley Cup Playoffs, and now it's on Briere to add around the edges of the roster. Vladar proved he can handle a decent work load and be there in big games, but it's a priority for the Flyers to add another capable one-B type goalie, who can push Vladar for the starter's crease.

Names floated include Joonas Korpisalo, Anthony Stolarz, and Jordan Binnington. None of them moves the needle the way a top-line center would, but you need both.

And the in-house contract list is mostly clean.

Brière said that he, as of now, has no worries about re-signing restricted free agents Zegras and defenseman Jamie Drysdale.

That is good news. Zegras's playoff performance pretty much settles the conversation about whether he is worth a long-term deal. Drysdale is twenty-three with top-pair upside. You keep both, you keep moving.

Around the leagues, and we have to start in Oklahoma City because what Victor Wembanyama did last night was the kind of thing that gets replayed for decades.

Victor Wembanyama had forty-one points and twenty-four rebounds, and Dylan Harper finished with twenty-four points and a team playoff-record seven steals as the San Antonio Spurs beat the Oklahoma City Thunder one hundred twenty-two to one hundred fifteen in a double-overtime classic to open the Western Conference Finals Monday night.

Wembanyama racked up forty-one points and twenty-four rebounds, becoming the youngest player in NBA history to record at least forty points and twenty rebounds in a playoff game. He became the first player since Wilt Chamberlain in 1960 to log at least forty points and twenty rebounds in his conference finals debut.

The context made it richer.

Wembanyama resisted the urge to dive deeply into the visual of watching Shai Gilgeous-Alexander accept an award he thought belonged to him just minutes before the biggest game of his NBA career. So, on the night Gilgeous-Alexander received his second Most Valuable Player award, Wembanyama snatched a consolation prize by way of a one-hundred-twenty-two to one-hundred-fifteen double-overtime triumph.

Gilgeous-Alexander, the freshly-minted back-to-back MVP, shot seven for twenty-three.

It was the sixth Game 1 in NBA playoff history to go into double overtime — the first since a Spurs-Warriors game in 2013.

Game two is tomorrow night. If you have any interest in basketball at all and you skipped this, find the replay. It might be the game of the year.
